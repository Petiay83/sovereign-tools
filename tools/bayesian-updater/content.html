📐 The Mathematical Intuition of Bayes

1. The Base Rate Fallacy

Humans intuitively judge probability by similarity, ignoring how rare the event is in the background. If a disease affects 1 in 10,000 people, even a test with 99% accuracy will produce 100 times more false positives than true positives.

2. Turing's Decibans (dB)

Alan Turing measured evidence in "bans" and "decibans" at Bletchley Park while breaking Enigma. Just like decibels in sound, evidence weights add up linearly: +10 dB multiplies odds by 10x, +20 dB multiplies odds by 100x, and -10 dB divides odds by 10x.

3. Conservation of Expected Evidence

You cannot anticipate being swayed in a specific direction before receiving evidence. If a positive test will make you believe $H$ more strongly, a negative test must proportionally make you believe $H$ less strongly.
