The Pharmacokinetics of Sleep Disruption

Caffeine works primarily as a competitive antagonist of adenosine \(A_1\) and \(A_{2A}\) receptors. Adenosine is the molecular byproduct of cellular energy expenditure (ATP breakdown) that builds up throughout your waking hours, creating "sleep pressure".

Even if you can fall asleep with 40mg of caffeine in your bloodstream, clinical polysomnography studies show it fragments sleep architecture, reduces Stage 3 & 4 non-REM slow-wave sleep (where physical repair and glymphatic brain clearance occur) by up to 20%, and increases micro-arousals.

How This Model Computes Decay

This calculator uses first-order pharmacokinetics with instantaneous gastrointestinal absorption (peaking at ~45 minutes):

C(t) = C₀ · (0.5)^(Δt / T½)

Where \(C₀\) is the initial bioavailable dose, \(\Delta t\) is hours elapsed since peak, and \(T½\) is the clearance half-life dictated by your CYP1A2 enzymatic phenotype.
