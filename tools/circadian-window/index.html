🧬 The Neurobiology Behind the Protocol

1. Melanopsin ipRGCs & SCN

Specialized retinal neurons containing melanopsin respond directly to blue-cyan wavelengths (~480 nm). They do not form visual images; instead, they send direct electrical signals via the retinohypothalamic tract to the suprachiasmatic nucleus (SCN), resetting peripheral clocks in the liver, heart, and adrenal glands.

2. Adenosine Kinetics & Crash

Adenosine builds up while awake, creating "sleep pressure". During groggy morning waking, residual adenosine lingers. If you consume caffeine immediately, it antagonizes adenosine receptors without clearing the molecule. Once caffeine degrades, a backlog of adenosine binds all at once, triggering an intense afternoon energy collapse.

3. Phase Response Curve & T_min

Your body temperature minimum ($T_{min}$) occurs ~90–120 min before waking. Exposing eyes to bright light after $T_{min}$ advances your circadian rhythm (makes you sleepy earlier and wake up earlier). Exposing eyes to bright light before $T_{min}$ delays your clock (makes you want to stay up later).
